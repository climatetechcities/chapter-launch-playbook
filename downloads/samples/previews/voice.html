Climate Tech Portland: how Sam writes

City: Portland Lead: Sam Rivera Built from: 4 samples (2 newsletter notes, 1 LinkedIn post, 1 email to volunteers) Last updated: 2026-08-04

Claude drafts the opening note, the picks and the housekeeping in this voice. To change it, send more writing or tell Claude what to change.

1. How they sound

  • Opens with one short, concrete sentence about the week or the weather, then gets to the point in the second.
  • Mostly short sentences. One longer one per paragraph, usually the one carrying the reason.
  • Always "we" for the chapter and "you" for the reader. "I" only for a personal aside.
  • Contractions throughout.
  • Dry humor in parentheses, at most once per note.
  • Recommends by saying what you will do there, not how good it will be.
  • Admits when a week is thin instead of dressing it up.

2. Openings and sign-offs

  • Greeting is "Hi all," on its own line. Seen in every sample.
  • First paragraph never starts with "Welcome" or the name of the newsletter.
  • Ends the note by handing off to the list, plainly.
  • Closes emails with "See you out there," (seen once).

3. Words and phrases they use

  • "worth the trip", "bring a friend", "low-key"
  • "if you only do one thing this week"
  • Names neighborhoods rather than addresses when talking about where something is.
  • Says "folks" for groups of people.

4. Things they never do

  • No exclamation marks in any sample.
  • Never says excited, thrilled, or amazing.
  • No rhetorical questions.
  • No hashtags, even on LinkedIn.

5. In their own words

It rained all week and the calendar is short. That's August.
If you only do one thing this week, make it the repair cafe. Bring the toaster you've been meaning to fix (we all have one).
We're looking for one more person to help with the newsletter. It's about an hour a week and you get to read everything first.
Low-key, free, and the folks who run it know every heat pump installer in town.

6. What the samples do not show

  • No sample of Sam writing about a paid or expensive event.
  • Only one sample longer than 150 words, so how they handle a long note is a guess.
