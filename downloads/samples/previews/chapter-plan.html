Climate Tech Portland

Launch plan

Monday, August 10, 2026 to Sunday, August 23, 2026

Bulleted lines are the steps the toolkit and the Climate Tech Cities team depend on. The grey notes are advice: take what is useful and skip the rest. You know your city better than this document does.

The full manual is the chapter launch playbook: https://climatetechcities.github.io/chapter-launch-playbook/. This plan is the short version with your dates in it.

Before you start

•     Create a project in Claude and name it Climate Tech Portland. Do everything for the chapter in chats inside that project.

•     Claude keeps your chapter files in that project and updates them itself. If it ever tells you it could not save one, it will hand you the file: add it to the project in project settings under add files, not attached to a chat.

•     Install the toolkit: the ctc-newsletter plugin. It includes chapter setup. The ctc-partner-map plugin is optional and can wait.

•     Install the Claude in Chrome extension. The weekly harvest needs it to read Luma and Eventbrite.

•     Turn on code execution in Settings, Capabilities.

 

The playbook's Install page has these in order with links. Skills only appear in a fresh conversation, so start a new one after installing.

Week 1

August 10 to August 16

Logo

•     Open the Logo step on the chapter launch playbook. It has the prompt with your city in it, the reference images to attach, and the full walkthrough.

•     Generate concepts in ChatGPT and pick one.

•     Send your pick to the Climate Tech Cities team for sign-off. Nothing with the logo on it goes public until it is approved: it ends up on the Substack, the website, and every event graphic, so changing it later means changing it everywhere.

 

Judge concepts at icon size. That is the size most people will ever see it at.

Substack

•     Once the logo is approved, send it and your chapter name (Climate Tech Portland) to the Climate Tech Cities team. They create the Substack publication and send you the login.

•     Confirm the web address with them at the same time. Your settings file records portlandclimatetech.substack.com, but there is no fixed rule yet, and once other chapters link to the address it cannot change quietly.

•     When you have the login, ask Claude in your project: "walk me through Substack setup" and "draft my welcome email". Both read your settings file.

 

Two settings worth doing early: the sender name as Sam Rivera rather than the chapter, because email from a person gets opened more often, and the welcome email, because Substack sends it to every new subscriber automatically and it is otherwise blank.

Week 2

August 17 to August 23

Source list

•     Say "run the newsletter" in your project. The first time, it builds your source list and your first list of opportunities before anything else. Paste in any event calendars or programs you already know when it asks.

•     Paste three to five things you have written when it asks: past notes, posts, emails. Claude learns how you write and drafts the personal parts of each issue to match. Optional, and worth doing.

•     Read the source list when it shows it to you. You live there and the research does not, so say what to add and what to drop. It is saved to your project once you say it looks right.

 

Every future issue is built from this list, so it is worth an hour now. Sources go quiet without telling anyone, which is why the schedule has a refresh every three months.

Subscriber list

•     Add your first subscribers in Substack and start with whoever you have. A first issue going to a few dozen people is normal.

 

Your own network, asked one by one, is usually most of the early list and converts better than a public post. If you are thinking of importing a list from somewhere, ask Claude "how do I build my list" first: there are consent rules, and stricter ones for chapters in the UK and EU.

First issue

Tuesday, August 18, 2026

•     Say "run the newsletter" in your project. Claude collects the events, shows you the list with a proposed cut and proposed picks, builds the issue from your reply, and hands it to you to finish.

 

A first issue is small and mostly a note about why the newsletter exists. That is the right shape, not a failure. Do not wait until there are more events.

Every week

The issue goes out every Tuesday. Two of the four steps are yours.

Step

What happens

Collect

Claude sweeps your sources and the reader submission form.

Review

Claude shows you every event with a proposed cut and proposed picks. You reply once with any changes.

Write

Claude builds the issue. You rewrite the opening note, the picks and the housekeeping in your own words.

Send

Paste into Substack, read it once, send.

 

Say "run the newsletter" and Claude runs these in order and stops where your input is needed. If you said yes to the weekly schedule, Claude starts by itself the day before your send day and you get a notification with the list ready to review. Keep your computer on with Claude open at that time so it can read the event calendars.

Every month

•     Nothing to remember. When your opportunities list is a month old, the weekly run refreshes it before it collects events, so the opportunities block has something in it in every issue.

Every quarter

•     When your source list is three months old, the weekly run offers to refresh it. Say yes. Sources go quiet without telling anyone, and the refresh catches a dead calendar before it thins out an issue.

Once the newsletter is running

•     Run ctc-city-resources and name your city. It researches the ecosystem and builds your resource workbook.

•     Then ctc-partner-list turns that workbook into a tiered list of who to approach and what to ask each one for.

•     Then ctc-partner-outreach drafts the messages in your own voice.

 

Partners are optional and have no deadline. Starting them before the newsletter is running is how leads end up finishing neither.

If you miss a week

•     Send the next issue on your usual send day. No apology or explanation needed. The target is weeks covered over a quarter, not consecutive weeks.
